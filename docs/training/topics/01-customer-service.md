# Module 1: Customer Service & Communication Skills

## Learning Objective

Develop professional communication skills to interact effectively with customers, carriers, and internal teams during incident management.

---

## Foundational Knowledge You Need

Before diving into this module, understand:

- **Customer service principles in technical environments**: Balancing empathy with accuracy.
- **Technical vs. non-technical communication**: How to explain complex issues without jargon.
- **Professional email structure**: Greeting, context, action items, closing.
- **Incident communication workflows**: Initial notification, status updates, resolution confirmation.

---

## Key Teaching Points

### 1. Translating Technical Issues for Customers

- Avoid acronyms unless the customer uses them first.
- Use analogies: "Think of your internet connection like a highway..."
- Focus on impact, not root cause: "Your phone service is down" vs. "SIP registration failed."
- Provide clear next steps and timelines.

### 2. Managing Expectations

- Be honest about what you know and don't know.
- Set realistic resolution timeframes (don't overpromise).
- Explain the escalation process: "I'm escalating this to our Tier 3 team."
- Commit to follow-up times and stick to them.

### 3. Tone and Empathy

- Match urgency: A complete outage requires a different tone than a minor feature request.
- Acknowledge frustration: "I understand this is impacting your business."
- Stay calm and professional, even if the customer is upset.
- Use positive language: "I'll get this resolved" vs. "I'll try."

### 4. Email Templates for Common Scenarios

#### Initial Outage Notification


Subject: Service Disruption – [Account/Service Name]

Hi [Customer Name],

We're currently investigating a service disruption affecting [specific service/location].
Our team is actively working to restore service.

Next update: [Time/Date]

If you have any questions, please reply to this email.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
NOC Tier 2 Technician

text

#### Status Update
Subject: Update – Service Disruption [Ticket #XXXXX]

Hi [Customer Name],

Our team has identified the root cause as [brief explanation].
We're implementing a fix and expect restoration by [Time/Date].

We'll continue to monitor and provide another update if anything changes.

Thank you for your patience.

Best regards,
[Your Name]

text

#### Resolution Confirmation
Subject: Resolved – Service Disruption [Ticket #XXXXX]

Hi [Customer Name],

Service has been fully restored as of [Time/Date].
We've confirmed [specific test] is working as expected.

A full incident report will be available within [timeframe].
Please let us know if you experience any further issues.

Best regards,
[Your Name]


### 5. Phone and Chat Communication

- Answer promptly and professionally: "Thank you for calling [Company], this is [Name]."
- Use active listening: "Let me make sure I understand..."
- Summarize before ending: "To confirm, I'll [action] and follow up by [time]."
- Document everything in the ticket immediately after the call.

## Hands-On Exercises

### Exercise 1: Rewrite Technical Emails

Take these 3 technical emails and rewrite them for a non-technical customer:

**Original:**
> "The SIP trunk is experiencing registration failures due to firewall NAT traversal issues. We're working on adjusting the ALG settings."

**Your rewrite:**
> (Write a customer-friendly version)

Repeat for 2 more examples.

---

### Exercise 2: Role-Play Call Simulation

Practice with a colleague or record yourself:

- Scenario: Customer calls about complete phone service outage.
- Goal: Explain the situation, set expectations, and commit to a follow-up time.
- Duration: 3–5 minutes.

Review your recording: Did you use clear language? Did you show empathy?

---

### Exercise 3: Create 5 Reusable Email Templates

Build templates for:

1. Initial outage notification
2. Scheduled maintenance notice
3. Status update during ongoing incident
4. Resolution confirmation
5. Escalation notification

Save these in a personal or team template library for quick reuse.

---

### Exercise 4: Tone Analysis

Read these 3 emails and identify tone issues:

1. Too technical
2. Too casual
3. Too vague

Rewrite each one to strike the right balance.

---

### Exercise 5: Build a Communication Checklist

Create a checklist you can use before sending any customer communication:

- [ ] Is the language clear and jargon-free?
- [ ] Did I set a clear expectation for next steps?
- [ ] Is the tone appropriate for the situation?
- [ ] Did I include a follow-up time?
- [ ] Did I proofread for errors?
